## 2. The six upgrades

### 2.1 Sheen and sparkle burst at each turn of the sway

**What the owner wants.** The figure sways ±16° yaw over 14 s (`swayYaw = 0.28 * sin(TAU * clock / 14)` in
`render()`, hero.js ~line 703). Each time it reaches one side and turns back, a light should sweep across the
figure ("blue shiny white"), and at the moment of the turn a burst of sparkles should fire all at once ("like
when we open a light"), several of them, in a colour that depends on the mode.

**Design.**

- **Trigger.** Detect the sway extremum: the sign change of `d(swayYaw)/dt`, i.e. every 7 s, alternating sides.
  Record `sheenAt = t` and `sheenDir = ±1` (the side it came from). Do not trigger from cursor tilt or touch
  drag; only from the base sway. Also fire once when the assembly finishes (`built ≈ 1.9 s`), so a visitor who
  never waits still sees it.
- **Sheen.** A specular band that travels across the figure in screen space over 1.1 s, direction `sheenDir`,
  easing `--ease-in-out`. In the vertex shader: `float sweep = u_sheen.x` (0→1 progress), band centre
  `bx = mix(box.left - w*0.2, box.right + w*0.2, sweep)`, weight `g = exp(-((px.x - bx) / (box.size * 0.08))^2)`,
  multiplied by the normal's facing (`pow(max(n.z, 0), 2)`) so it reads as light on a surface, not a stripe on a
  flat image. Effect: `v_hot = max(v_hot, g * 0.85)` with `u_hot` set to the mode's sheen colour, and
  `size *= 1 + 0.35 * g`. Keep the band thin and bright; never wash out the whole figure.
- **Sparkle burst.** At the trigger, choose ~40 dots on the lit half (seeded: `hash(id * 11u + burst)` <
  threshold, `n.z > 0.3`) and give each a star: it pops to 2.2× size and the sheen colour within 120 ms, then
  decays over 600–900 ms with a per-dot random delay of 0–250 ms so the burst reads as many lights coming on,
  not one flash. Draw the stars as 4-point crosses, not discs: in the fragment shader use
  `gl_PointCoord` to shape `a` with `max(disc, cross)` when `v_star > 0`. That is what makes them read as
  sparkles. Everything else stays round.
- **Colours per mode** (keep one hue family per mode, as the spec's one-accent rule):
  - light paper: sheen `#2C62FF` → white at the core (blue-white), stars `#2C62FF`;
  - dark paper: sheen cyan-white `#BFF4FF` with a `#38D9FF` fringe, stars `#8AE8FF`;
  - Gear Two: sheen warm white `#FFE9E0`, stars `#FF8A7A`, and the burst also triggers one glitch-tile frame
    (see 2.4), so the two systems feel like one.
- **Reduced motion:** no sheen, no bursts (static frame, as today). **Fallback still:** unchanged.
- **Performance:** all per-dot work stays in the vertex shader; the CPU only sets `u_sheen` (vec4: progress,
  direction, burst id, burst age) once per frame. Budget stays under 2 ms JS per frame (the readout under Fig. 0
  shows it).

**Tests.** Unit: a pure `sheenPhase(clock)` helper (export it) returns the trigger times and direction; assert
two triggers per 14 s period, alternating sign, and the first at assembly. e2e (hero-resilience): the reduced-
motion frame still hashes identically twice (existing test), `u_sheen` is `[0,0,0,0]` under reduced motion, and
with motion on, `u_sheen[0]` crosses 0→1 within 8 s of load. Add the sheen to the Gear Two section of the design
spec and to §8 Motion.

### 2.2 Face and hand detail

**Why it is weak.** The source is 424 px; the depth map is 424 px from Depth Anything V2 **Small**; the stipple
grid is at most 640 cells across the whole figure, so the face gets about 60 cells and a hand about 45. No shader
work fixes that; the data has to improve.

**Do this, in order, measuring after each step with 2× crops of the face and both hands at 1440 and 390**
(`DPR=2 CLIP=x,y,w,h node tools/qa/shots.mjs shot / 1440 900 light out.png`):

1. **Ask Aakash for the original render of the avatar** at its native size (it is an AI render; the original is
   likely ≥ 1024 px). If he has it, replace `assets/avatar.jpg` with a 1024 px (or larger) version. If not,
   upscale offline with Real-ESRGAN ×4 (`realesrgan-x4plus`) and inspect the face and hands by eye; reject it if
   it invents features. Keep the file name so the colophon and share images stay valid; rerun `npm run og`.
2. **Regenerate depth** with `python3 tools/hero/build.py --depth` using Depth Anything V2 **Base** (change the
   model id in `depth_map()`), at the new resolution. Compare `tools/hero/depth.png` before and after on the
   face: eye sockets, nose bridge, lips, and the fingers must separate.
3. **Raise the map size** `N` in `build.py` from 448 to 896 and `resolutionFor` in hero.js from
   `clamp(px * 0.8, 280, 640)` to `clamp(px * 1.1, 320, 960)`. Check the dot count (readout, `[data-dot-count]`;
   also `site.dots` in `tools/pages/site.json` and the colophon) and the 2 ms/frame budget; 50k dots at 1440 is
   fine on a GPU and still fine under SwiftShader in CI (the probe's `firstDraws[0].count > 5000` must hold).
   `data.png` must stay under the 200 KB total in `test/hero.test.cjs` (it checks data.png + bluenoise.png +
   hero.json + color.webp): at 896 px use `optimize=True` and, if needed, quantise depth to 7 bits.
4. **Region weighting.** In `maps()`, multiply `ink` by a detail weight: `1 + 0.6 * local_contrast` where
   `local_contrast = |lum - blurf(lum, 3)|` normalised, so the face, the hair, the fingers, and the caduceus
   snakes get denser dots than flat marble. Cap at 1.6. Keep `DENSITY` so the total count moves by < 20%.
5. **Two sizes of dot.** Store `detail` in `a_p.w`'s upper range (ink is 0..1; pack `ink * 0.75 + detail * 0.25`)
   or add a byte to `a_c` — and in the shader shrink dots where detail is high (`size *= mix(1.0, 0.7, detail)`)
   so fine features are drawn with small dense dots and broad shadows with larger sparse ones. This is the
   engraving convention and the single biggest legibility win for faces.
6. Re-render `still.webp` and `preview.webp` (build.py does it) and look at the no-WebGL fallback at 390.

Acceptance: at 1440 light and dark, a viewer can read the eyes, nose, and mouth of the statue and count five
fingers on the raised hand in a 2× crop. The face must not become a grey blob under the colour layer.

### 2.4 Glitch tiles through all of Gear Two ("the squared blink")

**Why it does not show.** The tiles only run inside the glitch window: `glitchUntil = performance.now() + 520`
on `data-phase` flash/glitch (hero.js ~814), so they appear for half a second when Gear Two switches on or off
and never again. The owner wants them as part of Gear Two's living state.

**Design.** Keep the switch-on burst as is. While Gear Two is on, add an intermittent tear:

- Every heartbeat, with probability 0.35 (seeded by the beat index so it is deterministic per session clock),
  schedule a tear of 2–4 frames at 24 fps starting at the beat's first peak (`heartbeat(t) > 0.8`), with 1–2
  tiles (`glitchTiles(frame, box, 2, onFigure)`), slices on (`u_glitch` 0.6, lower than the switch-on's 1.0),
  no afterimages. About every third beat the figure "stutters" for 100 ms; between tears it is clean.
- Every ~6 s (seeded), a longer tear of 8 frames with 3 tiles and one afterimage pass, aligned to the sheen
  trigger from 2.1 when both fall within a second of each other.
- The 2D overlay keeps drawing the tile frames (`drawTiles`). Make the frame 1 px, accent, 0.9 alpha, and draw a
  second, source-position frame at 0.35 alpha so the tear reads as "moved from here to there".
- Never during reduced motion; never on light or dark (only Gear Two); never while a touch drag is active.

**Tests.** Unit: a pure `tearSchedule(beatIndex)` helper (export) is deterministic and yields ~35% of beats.
e2e: in Gear Two, within 6 s, `u_glitch` is observed > 0 at least once after the switch-on window has closed
(poll the probe's `uniforms.u_glitch`), and remains `[0]` for the whole period in light mode and under reduced
motion.

### 2.6 Redesign the colour

The owner finds the current colours unappealing. They are sampled from the blurred avatar, which makes them
muddy (the gold reads as brown-orange; the body picks up grey-cream; the blue at the base is pale). Replace
sampling with **a designed palette applied through a material map**:

1. **Material map** (offline, in `build.py`, saved as `assets/hero/color.webp` still, so the engine's loading
   path stays): segment the avatar inside the figure mask into five materials by hue and luminance with a
   small k-means (k = 5) seeded with: gold (wings, caduceus, hair), marble (body), cloud (pink/peach base),
   lightning (cyan at the base), sky-glint (the blue nebula glints on the wings). Store the material index in R
   (0..4 × 51), a soft 0..1 weight for how strongly the pixel belongs to it in G, the local sparkle in A as
   today, B free for `detail` (2.2). Blur the index boundaries 2 px so edges do not flicker between materials.
2. **Palette per mode**, in the shader as a `uniform vec3 u_palette[5]` set from CSS custom properties so the
   design lives in `site.css` (`--mat-gold`, `--mat-marble`, …, per theme), and the colophon and design spec
   can quote them:
   - light: gold `#B8860B` → leaf-gold `#D4A017` highlights, marble = ink (#0C0C0C) — keep the engraving,
     cloud `#C76B6B` (rose, deep enough for white paper), lightning `#1F7FD6`, glint `#2C62FF`;
   - dark: gold `#F2C14E` with `#FFE08A` highlights, marble = warm white ink, cloud `#F0A8B8`, lightning
     `#55E6FF`, glint `#8AB4FF`;
   - Gear Two: everything red as today, except gold → `#FF9E80` at 40% weight so the wings still read as a
     different material; marble stays `#FF3B30`.
   Mix `v_color = mix(ink, palette[material], weight * u_tint)`; let `lam` (the lighting term) move each
   material between its base and highlight shade: `mix(base, highlight, lam)`.
3. **Judge it** on the three 2× crops (figure, wings, face) in all three modes and at 390. The test of success
   is that the gold reads as gold leaf, the clouds as rose, not salmon, and the marble keeps every bit of the
   engraving contrast it has in the monochrome version. If the palette fights the blue accent of the ring on
   light paper, lower the glint and lightning saturation first; never change the accent.
4. Keep `sampleColors` and its tests (they sample whatever is in the map); add a unit test that `materialMap`
   (if you add a JS helper) or the shipped `color.webp` has exactly five index levels inside the mask.

Document the palette in `docs/design-spec.md` §5 (a small table) and §7, and in the colophon notes.

## 3. Traps you will hit (all found the hard way)

- **Canvas 2D premultiplies alpha.** `loadImageData` draws the image to a 2D canvas; any RGBA pixel with alpha 0
  loses its RGB. That is why `color.webp` keeps alpha ≥ 128. Keep the floor if you reuse alpha.
- **WebP drops colour under alpha 0** unless saved with `exact=True` (Pillow). Already set in `color_map()`.
- **IntersectionObserver respects `clip-path`.** A fully clipped element never intersects, so reveals clip the
  child (`img`, `video`), not the observed frame.
- **The generator must reproduce every page byte for byte**, and the colophon prints gzipped CSS+JS sizes and
  test counts: regenerate after every change to CSS, JS, or tests, and commit the regenerated `index.html`.
- **One program, one buffer, one VAO.** `test/e2e/hero-resilience.test.mjs` counts them per context restoration
  and compares the buffer upload's bytes and hash across restorations, so geometry must be deterministic
  (seeded hashes only, no `Math.random`).
- **Reduced motion draws exactly one still** and two stills must hash identically: nothing time-based may leak
  into a reduced-motion frame (`u_time` is 0, keep every new uniform constant there).
- **Uniform names are part of the test contract:** the probe records `u_rot`, `u_time`, `u_blink`, `u_beat`,
  `u_glitch`, `u_pointer`, `u_rip`, `u_build`, `u_tint`. Keep their meanings; add new ones freely.
- **`--beat-delay`** is written once when Gear Two switches on, from `performance.now()`; any new CSS pulse
  must use `animation-delay: var(--beat-delay, 0s)` with a 0.9 s period to stay in phase with the dots.
- **The grid-alignment e2e test** checks every `.row > *` (and a few more selectors) at four widths; new blocks
  go on a major line, a minor line, or the inset beside one (see `.matrix` for the arithmetic).
- **axe runs on every page at four widths in three modes**, with best-practice rules: no ARIA role on an `<a>`
  (wrap it), labelled regions, one `h1`.
- **SwiftShader.** This machine class renders WebGL in software. Phase timings are wall-clock and stay exact
  (`/tmp/timing.mjs`-style check: observe `data-phase` mutations with `performance.now()`), but recordings run
  at a few frames per second; judge motion by the numbers and by held states, not by the video's rhythm.
- **Lenis** owns scrolling; `main` gets a `transform` only during the glitch jitter. Do not add transforms to
  `main` at rest or `position: fixed` children inside it.
- **The `.gear` label never changes** and the accent is the only interactive colour; Gear Two's red is the one
  exception by design (§5, §9 of the spec).
